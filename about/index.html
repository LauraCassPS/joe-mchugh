About Joe

A few things I've learned along the way

I've spent much of my working life with people. As a teacher, youth and community worker, and for 25 years in politics, including serving as Minister for Education during Covid-19.

I've had the privilege of working with people from all walks of life, building teams, making difficult decisions and dealing with challenges that don't always come with straightforward answers.

And if there's one thing I've learned, it's that experience doesn't mean you've finished learning.

Stories don't have to define what happens next

In 2014, I was appointed Minister of State with responsibility for the Gaeltacht and the Irish language. There was one small complication. My Irish wasn't exactly up to the job.

I could have continued believing what I'd told myself since school, that I was no good at languages. Instead, I decided to do something about it.

Learning for me as a child and teenager was a box ticking exercise. Quite literally. Watching the seconds tick away on the clock was my thing. I was on the countdown. Waiting for the bell. I wanted it all to be over.

Things changed when I went back to learning Gaeilge. This time was different. I was having fun and people got behind me. I was also leaving myself vulnerable in the eyes of the public.

But sure look. What did that matter? I knew I was going to make mistakes. And plenty of them.

That experience led to a book, Beidh Tú Alright, and eventually a one-man stage show, Joe's Call.

A different level of vulnerability! As a close friend of mine said, "I was about to make a show of myself again". Literally!

I'd always been curious about the stage. And you know what, it helped me in my transition from politics.

When I think back to the stories I repeatedly told myself about what I could or couldn't do, I realise now that they were just stories.

And stories don't have to define what happens next.

Beidh Tú Alright

An Irish Language Journey

I wrote this book to share what happened when I went back to something I thought I couldn’t do. There were mistakes. Plenty of them. But there was also laughter, encouragement and a connection with the language and its people that I hadn’t expected.

If you’ve ever told yourself it’s too late to learn something new, you might recognise a bit of yourself in it.

The power of Connections

Growing up on a farm in Donegal in the 1970s, I learned the importance of people coming together.

There is an Irish tradition known as the meitheal, where neighbours helped one another gather potatoes, cut and thresh corn, bring home turf and do whatever else needed doing. I can't remember 'doing the dishes' being included though!

There was no payment involved. It was simply how things worked, and there was a real sense of purpose in it.

Looking back, I can see how much that experience influenced me. Throughout my life, I've enjoyed bringing people together and seeing what happens as a result.

When I was a youth worker, I came across the idea of a democratically elected youth council in Lyon, France, and decided to introduce it in Donegal when I was a member of Donegal County Council.

Young people stood for election, campaigned and competed for places on the council. It was the first of its kind in Ireland, and it's still going today.

I love bumping into former members and hearing about the campaigns they championed.

Back in 1995, when I was living in Dubai, I was involved in setting up Dubai Celts, the first Gaelic football team in the Middle East. My Dubai Creek soccer team generously allowed us to use their pitch.

After a few pints I always claim this paved the way for the opening of Croke Park to other sports. As I said, 'after a few pints…'!

You never quite know where those connections might lead.

Knowing when to ask a different question

?

Politics taught me a great deal about leadership, responsibility and the expectations that come with both.

People came to me with problems, and understandably, they wanted solutions. There was enormous satisfaction in being able to make a difference.

But after 25 years, I was curious about what else I might do.

That curiosity eventually brought me to coaching, where I discovered a different way of working with people.

Looking back, listening, bringing people together and encouraging different voices have been part of my life for a long time. Coaching has given me another way to do that.

Castar na daoine ar a chéile ach ní chastar na cnoic nó na sléibhte.

People meet, but the hills and mountains don't.

A few more things I've learned

On health, gratitude and second chances

When I was burning the candle at both ends, with minimal sleep, poor diet and lack of exercise, I believed the story I was telling myself: 'at least I'm not sick'.

And then I got sick. Twice.

A story of two hospitals, one in Dublin and one in Derry. Two incredible teams came to the rescue.

That was when I started taking gratitude seriously for the first time. I am one of the lucky ones. I came through it and I am grateful for the help and support and that I'm getting a second chance.

I still enjoy my pint, my lemon meringue and pain au chocolat from time to time. This time though, I'm more conscious about the importance of exercising my mind and my body.

My three best teachers

While they may disagree wholeheartedly, I am learning something new every day from three teenagers who live under the same roof as me.

Their wisdom at times astonishes me. They keep me grounded and call me out when I need to be called out, which is often!

From a work point of view, it reinforces my educational philosophy that every voice should contribute to a learning outcome.

Letting the hair down (what's left of it!)

Falling from a balcony in the USA in 1992 and doing a job on my knee ligaments is a tempting excuse to give up the ghost of staying fit.

I'm not giving up without a fight.

My daily exercise routine, which includes Qigong (thanks William McElhinney), and my regular dip in the Atlantic Ocean with the 'splashers and dashers' is helping me to keep going.

Hats off to all the organisers of the many Park Runs I have participated in. I am indebted to you.

My philosophy

I'm a realist. I have good days and bad days. There will be ups and downs. And as the man says, 'we're all heading in the same direction'.

I work hard at living in the present. Yesterday is gone and that ship has sailed.

Tomorrow? How do you make God laugh? Tell him/her about your plans!

The moment I get out of bed, I'm in control. I get to decide. I'm in charge of me.

Let's rumble!

A little more about me

Joe McHugh is an EMCC-accredited Senior Practitioner Coach/Mentor, former Government Minister and author of Beidh Tú Alright: An Irish Language Journey.

His background includes teaching, youth and community work, 25 years in Irish politics, and an MSc in Positive Health Science from RCSI.

He lives in Donegal with his wife Olwyn and family, and continues to pursue his interest in the Irish language, lifelong learning and the people and communities around him.

Bringing people together

His parliamentary roles included:

  • Co-chair of the British–Irish Parliamentary Assembly
  • Chair of the Good Friday Agreement Implementation Committee
  • Chair of the European Affairs Committee
